1. Introduction to Casting and Moulding

Definition, primary materials, and the 6 basic steps

PDF Page 2

Casting is one of the oldest and most versatile manufacturing processes. It involves pouring molten liquid metal into a pre-formed cavity (the mould) and allowing it to solidify. The solidified metal takes the exact shape of the cavity, yielding a product known as a casting[cite: 37].

Moulding is the preparatory step where the required cavity is created inside a refractory material (typically sand). The mould must accurately replicate the final shape, withstand high temperatures, and allow the metal to flow and solidify without defects[cite: 37]. Casting is widely preferred for producing complex shapes, large components, and parts that are difficult to machine[cite: 37].

2. Patterns in Foundry

Materials, Types, and Pattern Allowances

PDF Pages 2–4

A Pattern is a replica of the final casting used to form the mould cavity. It determines the exact external shape, establishes the parting line, incorporates the gating system (sprue, runner, riser), and accounts for various physical allowances[cite: 37].

Pattern Materials[cite: 38]
MaterialPropertiesTypical Use
WoodLightweight, cheap, low durabilitySmall batches, prototypes
Metals (Al, Cast Iron, Brass)Durable, accurate, wear-resistant, heavyMass production, long runs
Plastics (Epoxy)Lightweight, smooth, dimensionally stableMedium to large-scale
Plaster/WaxVery fine surface finish, burns out easilyInvestment casting (lost-wax)

Common Types of Patterns[cite: 38]

  • Solid/Single-piece: Simplest type for simple shapes.
  • Split Pattern: Divided into two halves along the parting line (drag and cope).
  • Match-plate: Halves mounted on opposite sides of a metal plate. Efficient for machines.
  • Cope and Drag: Halves separately mounted on respective flasks for very large castings.
  • Gated Pattern: Gating system (runners/gates) integrally mounted to save time.
  • Sweep Pattern: Wooden board rotated around central axis (e.g., for large bells).
  • Segmental: Large circular shapes moulded in separate segments.
  • Loose-piece: Features separate loose pieces for easy removal when undercuts exist.

Pattern Allowances[cite: 39]

1. Shrinkage Allowance: All metals shrink on cooling. Pattern made 1-2% larger to compensate for volumetric contraction.

2. Draft Allowance: A slight taper (0.5°-3°) on vertical surfaces so the pattern withdraws smoothly without damaging mould edges.

3. Machining Allowance: Extra metal added to surfaces to be machined (shafts, bores) for a clean finish later.

4. Distortion (Camber) Allowance: For long, U-shaped parts, pattern is curved oppositely so it straightens as it cools.

5. Rapping Allowance: A negative allowance; pattern is made slightly smaller because rapping (hammering) during extraction enlarges the cavity.

3. Mould Making & Sand

Sand Constituents, Types, and Essential Mould Properties

PDF Pages 4–6

Anatomy of a Sand Mould[cite: 37, 39]

Moulding Sand Ingredients[cite: 41]

  • Silica Sand (70-85%): The refractory base. Withstands high temps without melting.
  • Clay / Bentonite (10-15%): The binder. Becomes plastic with water to provide "green strength".
  • Water / Moisture (3-5%): The catalyst. Activates the clay and provides steam to help gases escape.
  • Coal Dust: Prevents burn-in by forming a reducing gas layer, improving finish.
  • Wood Flour: Burns off during pouring, leaving microscopic voids for collapsibility/permeability.
  • Iron Oxide: Improves refractoriness and prevents expansion defects like scabbing.

Types of Moulding Sand[cite: 40]

  • Green Sand: Most common. Moist, unbaked silica + clay + water.
  • Dry Sand: Green sand baked at 150-300°C to remove moisture. Used for larger castings.
  • Loam Sand: Fine sand with very high clay (30-50%) and water, forming a paste.
  • Facing Sand: Pure, fine sand placed directly against the pattern for smooth finish.
  • Backing (Floor) Sand: Cheaper, reused sand filling the rest of the flask.
  • Parting Sand: Dry silica/limestone powder to prevent cope and drag from sticking.

6 Essential Properties of a Good Sand Mould[cite: 41, 42]

1. Porosity / Permeability: Allows gases, steam, and air to escape to prevent blowholes.
2. Green Strength: Strength in its moist state to hold shape after pattern withdrawal.
3. Dry Strength: Strength after moisture evaporates; withstands metallostatic pressure.
4. Refractoriness: Withstands high temperature of molten metal without fusing.
5. Flowability: Ability to pack tightly under ramming to reproduce intricate details.
6. Collapsibility: Ability to break down easily after solidification so casting can shrink safely.

4. Melting, Pouring & Solidification

Muffle Furnace operation, pouring guidelines, and shake-out

PDF Pages 7–8

Melting (Muffle Furnace)[cite: 42]

Used primarily for non-ferrous metals (Al, brass, bronze). It is an indirect-heating furnace. The metal sits in a graphite crucible inside the muffle chamber.

Why indirect? Ensures molten metal is not exposed to combustion gases or flame, minimizing oxidation, gas absorption, and contamination.

Pouring[cite: 42, 43]

  • Skimming: Dross (oxides/impurities) is skimmed off the surface before pouring.
  • Ladle Pre-heating: Prevents thermal shock and moisture condensation.
  • Stream: Steady, directed straight down the sprue to avoid splashing.
  • Clamping: Mould must be weighted down to resist upward buoyant force.

Solidification & Shake-out[cite: 43]

  • Progressive Freezing: Solidification starts at mould walls and moves inward.
  • Directional Solidification: Casting should freeze such that the riser remains liquid longest to feed shrinkage.
  • Shake-out: Cope is lifted, casting is knocked out of drag sand. Rough casting is sent for fettling/finishing.

5. Casting Defects & Remedies

Categorized by Gas, Shrinkage, Mould Material, Pouring, and Metallurgical

PDF Pages 8–10
Category Specific Defect & Description[cite: 44, 45] Common Remedies[cite: 45]
1. Gas Defects • Blowholes: Smooth, spherical/elongated cavities.
• Pinholes: Small, uniform pores (common in Al alloys).
• Scabs: Rough projections caused by gas lifting sand. 		Improve venting, reduce moisture in sand, increase permeability.
2. Shrinkage • Shrinkage Cavity (Pipe): Open/internal void at top due to insufficient feeding.
• Micro-porosity: Fine voids between dendrites. 		Provide adequate riser size/placement, use chills for directional solidification.
3. Mould Material • Cuts/Washes: Scalloped depressions from sand erosion.
• Swell: Enlargement due to insufficient mould strength.
• Drop: Projection caused by sand breaking off cope.
• Rat-tail/Buckle: V-shaped cracks from sand expansion. 		Increase ramming density, improve sand strength, use better binder.
4. Pouring/Filling • Misrun: Incomplete filling leaving rounded edges (temp too low).
• Cold Shut: Two streams meet but fail to fuse, leaving a line.
• Shifting (Mismatch): Cope and drag misaligned. 		Increase pouring temperature, design proper gating, ensure smooth flow.
5. Metallurgical • Hot Tears (Cracks): Jagged tears during cooling due to restrained contraction.
• Segregation: Non-uniform alloy distribution. 		Modify mould collapsibility, adjust alloy composition, control cooling rate.
