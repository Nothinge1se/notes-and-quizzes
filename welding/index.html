1. Definition & Overview of Welding

Coalescence, Metallurgical Bonds, and Fusion vs. Solid-State

PDF Page 1 • Lines 5–22

What is Welding? (Page 1, Lines 5–10)
Welding is a permanent materials-joining process that produces coalescence (fusion) of workpieces by heating them to a suitable temperature. It can occur with or without pressure, and with or without filler material.

The fundamental principle involves melting the faying surfaces of the base materials so they intermix and, upon cooling, form a strong metallurgical bond. This high-temperature melting distinguishes welding from lower-temperature processes like brazing and soldering.

Fusion Welding

  • Key Feature: Melts the base materials without external pressure.
  • Examples:
    • Arc Welding (MMAW, GMAW/MIG, GTAW/TIG, SAW)
    • Gas Welding (Oxy-Acetylene)
    • Energy Beam (Laser, Electron Beam)

Solid-State Welding

  • Key Feature: Joining occurs via heat and pressure without melting the base material.
  • Examples:
    • Friction Welding
    • Ultrasonic Welding
    • Diffusion Bonding
    • Forge Welding

2. Manual Metal Arc Welding (MMAW) Fundamentals

Cross-sectional anatomy of the shielded metal arc process

PDF Page 2 • Lines 1–11

MMAW (or SMAW) is a manual arc welding process that uses a consumable electrode coated with flux. The intense heat of the arc (3,000°C to 4,000°C) melts both the electrode and the base metal. The coating decomposes to provide a protective gaseous shield and a slag blanket over the hot weld metal.

3. Electrode Classification & Functions of Flux

AWS Codes (E7018) and the 7 critical roles of the flux coating

PDF Page 2 (Lines 12–35) & Page 3 (Lines 1–9)

AWS Classification Breakdown

E 70 1 8
  • E
    Electrode: Indicates a consumable arc welding electrode.
  • 70
    Tensile Strength: Minimum tensile strength in thousands of psi (e.g., 70,000 psi).
  • 1
    Welding Position: 1 = All positions. (2 = Flat/Horizontal only).
  • 8
    Coating/Current: 8 = Low hydrogen, AC/DC+. (0 = High cellulose, DC+).
Cellulosic (EXX10): Deep penetration, highly gaseous shield.
Rutile (EXX20): Smooth arc, good slag detachability.
Basic/Low-Hydrogen (EXX18): Excellent crack resistance, for high-strength steels.

7 Critical Functions of Flux

1. Arc Stabilization Contains ionizing elements (potassium, sodium) to maintain a steady arc.
2. Gaseous Shielding Produces CO₂/Hydrogen to displace O₂/N₂, preventing porosity.
3. Slag Formation Melts to float on the pool, protecting metal from air.
4. De-oxidation Ferro-manganese/silicon removes dissolved oxygen from molten metal.
5. Alloying Adds Ni, Cr, Mo to improve mechanical properties and hardenability.
6. Bead Shape Control Influences fluidity and surface tension for a smooth profile.
7. Insulation Slag slows down cooling, allowing trapped gases to escape and reducing hard zone formation.

5. Five Fundamental Types of Welding Joints

As classified by the American Welding Society (AWS)

PDF Page 3 • Lines 26–34 & Page 4 (Lines 1–2)
1. Butt Joint

Two parts in the same plane joined edge-to-edge.

2. Lap Joint

Two overlapping parts joined on outside surfaces (fillet).

3. T-Joint

One part placed at a 90° right angle to another.

4. Corner Joint

Forms an "L" shape at the edge of both plates.

5. Edge Joint

Plates placed side-by-side, welded on the edge surface.

6. AC Welding vs. DC Welding

Characteristics based on polarity, arc blow, and applications

PDF Page 4 • Lines 4–24
Parameter AC Welding (Alternating Current) DC Welding (Direct Current)
Current & Polarity Alternates direction (50/60 Hz). No designated polarity (continuous change). Flows one way. DCEN (Straight) = Electrode Negative. DCEP (Reverse) = Electrode Positive.
Arc Blow Minimal to none; not affected by magnetic fields. Susceptible to arc blow (deflection) at high currents.
Cost & Stability Cheaper transformers; high efficiency. Less stable arc; spatter issues; needs high OCV. More expensive (rectifiers). Very stable, smooth arc, easier to strike and maintain.
Penetration Medium, moderate penetration. DCEN: Deeper penetration.
DCEP: Higher heat on work, shallow penetration (cleaning action).
Applications Flat/horizontal positions, large industrial installations. Rutile/basic electrodes. Positional welding (vertical/overhead), thin sheets, critical jobs. Low-hydrogen electrodes.

7. Safety Precautions in the Welding Shop

PPE, Environmental, Electrical, Fire, and Gas safety

PDF Page 4 (Lines 25–35) & Page 5 (Lines 1–23)

A. PPE

  • Helmet shade lens (minimum #10).
  • Leather gloves, aprons, sleeves.
  • High-top, steel-toe safety boots.
  • No synthetic fabrics; use 100% cotton/wool.

B. Environmental

  • Mechanical fume extraction for toxic gases (ozone).
  • P3 masks for galvanized/stainless steel.
  • Clear combustibles within a 35-foot (10m) radius.

C. Electrical

  • Inspect cables daily for worn insulation.
  • Never use wet gloves or stand on wet floors.
  • Properly ground power source.
  • Switch off when changing electrodes.

D. Fire & Gas

  • CO₂/dry chemical extinguisher accessible.
  • Stubs in designated metal containers.
  • Mark workpiece "HOT"; 30 min fire watch.
  • Store cylinders upright, chained. Keep oil/grease away from oxygen.

8. Procedure: Making a Double V Butt Joint

Step-by-step for thick plates (>16mm): Edge Prep, Root Pass, and Back Gouging

PDF Page 5 (Lines 24–28) & Pages 6-7
1. Edge Preparation

60°-70° included angle. Tacked every 150-200mm.

2. Root Pass (Side A)

E6010/E7018. Weaving/stringer technique for full penetration.

3. Back Gouging (Side B)

Crucial: Remove initial root pass to sound metal to avoid defects.

Step 4: Filling & Capping (Side A): Remove slag. Deposit filler passes (overlapping 50%). Final capping pass needs 1-2mm reinforcement, ripple-free.

Step 6: Welding Side B: Clean the gouged U-groove. Deposit new root pass to fuse with Side A. Fill and cap identically.

Step 7: Interpass Cleaning & Temp: Wire-brush every 2-3 passes. Maintain interpass temperature (e.g., <250°C for low-alloy steels) to avoid hydrogen cracking.

Step 8: Inspection: Cool slowly. Perform NDT (Radiography/UT). Perfect joint = complete fusion, symmetrical reinforcement, no undercut.
