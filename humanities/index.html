Module 1: Interaction with Human Values

Harmony between Self and the Body

Any human existence we think of as comprised of two separate constructs: the self and the body. The body is the shell or the receptacle that houses the self. The body carries the soul and the spirit within the soul, and it is this soul and spirit that carries consciousness, and it is this consciousness that leads to the framing of the shell. So, on one hand soul and body are two distinct constructs and on the other they overlap.

Going by the parameter of being distinct, it can be said that the needs of the self are qualitative in nature. As the self embodies paraphernalia of basic human aspirations, which desires to be in continuous happiness and prosperities, the needs of the body on the other hand are physical facilities and are quantitative in nature. Values that Frame the Self

1. Human values: The values that are inculcated from home within us right from the time when we start developing our conscience are considered to be the basic human values which we carry forward all through our life in order to comply with the laws and precepts set by institutions.

2. Social values: The values that we as human beings integrate as a part of the society are social values. These values vary according to context and situations depending on the feedback and judgement of others.

3. Spiritual values: Spiritual values are the ones that cannot be mixed with religious values because spirit is something that is supposed to be internalized by an individual and that can be something within or maybe external forces beyond god, which can include even something like the nature.

4. Religious values: Religious values are the ones that are initiated from home or family in majority of the cases. These values are dogmatic in nature and thus different from spiritual values.

5. Ethical values: Ethical values differ from person to person working as catalyst in supporting a person putting his or her full potential in a situation which demands utmost display of Ethicality.

6. Environmental values: Environmental values though are not as such initiated from home but we get sensitized about it as we grow older, by seeing the hazards the planet is facing because of hurling harm upon the environment.

Having spoken about the values, it is important to mention that the self, besides possessing the aforesaid values, also houses three very important modes of desire, thought and expectations.

Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs

Maslow’s hierarchy of needs theory explained through the pyramid.

Abraham Maslow is an American psychologist who proposed the hierarchy of needs theory in a paper called “Theory of Human Motivation” in the year 1945. He explained the theory with the aid of a pyramid in which he has put the human needs in several tiers, with the physiological needs occupying the lowest rung of the ladder and transcendence at the top. With the help of the pyramid he tries to explain how human beings evolve in real life passing through the different levels of needs.

Cooley’s Looking Glass Self Theory

Looking glass self theory, proposed by Charles Horton Cooley, the American sociologist-psychologist, in the year 1930 in a book called “Human Nature & Social Order”, tried to explain that human behaviour conforms to the assessment done by the society after getting to experience human attitude through different actions. Based on this Cooley proposed 3 stages of social development in human being:

Stage (i)Imagination appearance
Stage (ii)Imagination judgement
Stage (iii)Self feeling acting upon the judgements

Consequentialism

Consequentialism along with eudaimonism comes under the umbrella of teleological ethics, a group of views which claims that the intrinsic value of any act lies in its capacity to produce things of greater value. Consequentialism is a theory that associates itself with teleological ethics claiming that it is the consequence of any action that decides whether the action has been right or wrong. This is a theory that was proposed by John Stuart Mill and Jeremy Bentham in the 18th century. Countering this rationale, another school of thoughts emerged which threw light on the fact that it is the action which is morally or immorally guided, the consequence is not the determiner of the action. This theory was proposed by Immanuel Kant.

Consequentialism for a long time showcased its predominance as one of the social action theories and was studied under the heads of utilitarianism and hedonism. Utilitarianism is about understanding about a consequence which decided whether the action has done greater good to a greater number of people or not. Hedonism on the other hand is about understanding whether the consequence fetched pleasure or pain to a particular class or people of society.

Deontological Ethics (Non-Consequentialism)

Refuting the theory of consequentialism proposed by John Stuart Mill and Jeremy Bentham, Immanuel Kant, another great philosopher of 18th century, proposed the concept of non-consequentialism which is also known as deontological ethics. The word deontology is derived from the root word ‘deon’. The word ‘deon’ means duty and the theory states that it is the action which is most important and the action is either morally or immorally guided. The consequence, in the deontological context, is guided by the action. Deontological ethics is the kind that is followed by all and sundry in general. The workplace ethics that we talk about or are concerned here with is what rests on deontological ethics. It is the duty ethics that has led to the brewing of canons of ethics and codes of ethics.

Canons of Ethics

Canons are the rules that are generally set by an organization, expected to be followed by the employees working under a particular umbrella. The broad heads of the canons are:

1) Confidentiality

Any organization we talk about adopt certain business strategies in order to move ahead and carry on with the business keeping the good will intact. In such cases the organization should maintain keeping the strategy sacrosanct. Not only higher business strategies but also smaller decisions which are supposed to be kept confined within a few should not be divulged.

2) Diligence

All the stake holders in any organization should put in utmost effort to keep the business going in the right direction. Putting in either intelligence smart work or hard work. The business is the primary concern and working diligently to achieve good business should be the objective of the employees.

3) Integrity

Integrity is the attribute of integrating honesty, hard work and several other traits which are pre-requisites for conducting a business in a right way, spearheading the business to become a profit driven, value based organization.

4) Fairness

Fairness is another pre-requisite for motivating the employees in an organization to work for it in the most infallible fashion. Unless there is fairness with regard to salary and promotion in particular. The employees will not be motivated to work in a diligent manner.

5) Legality

Any organization that we think about is bound by rules and laws and so are the employees. If the organization at its own level and even if the employees fail to adhere to the laws and the precepts, both the organization and the employees are supposed to be taken to task with punitive actions.

6) Loyalty

Any organization and its employees are supposed to comply with the value-based and compliance-based code of ethics, thereby, remaining loyal to the system and to the clients as well.

7) Non-discrimination

Organizations are supposed to be non-discriminatory with regard to hiring, motivating and retaining employees. Many employees complain about discriminatory approaches of employers, particularly, with regard to gender discrimination and discrimination arising out of nepotism. Any organization should do away with these kind of vices because these serve as impediments in the growth of the business.

Apart from the canons an organization should also be relying on following the compliance-based code of ethics and value-based code of ethics.

Code of Ethics

Dated 22.9.26 in the notes.

Codes of Ethics are certain principles that govern the right way of conducting a business. Code of Ethics is principally of two types. One is compliance-based code of ethics, other is value based code of ethics.

Compliance-based code of ethics

Compliance based code of ethics are laws and policies to ensure hiring, safety and security of employees and policies for dismissing or firing people from job. Compliance based code of ethics are designed or set keeping in mind the labour laws, particularly when it is concerned about the hiring and safety security of the employees. With regard to maintaining a strong good will in the market and a strong clientele as well, a robust business system with experienced employees is a basic necessity.

Value based code of ethics

For any business to be run smoothly it is important to comply with the value-based code of ethics, because value forms the backbone of a strong good will. Sometimes the organizations consider a particular product a cash cow of minting money and they off and on adopt a wrong means of escalating the scale of product so that the business turnover remains impressive no matter what harm it hurls upon the buyers or clients. This unfair way of conducting a business soon takes a toll on the trajectory of the organization.

One-Shot Summary

Everything above in bullet points, for a quick revision.

Module 1: Interaction with Human Values

  • Human existence = self + body. Body is the shell that houses the self (soul, spirit, consciousness). Distinct but overlapping.
  • Needs of the self are qualitative (happiness, prosperity). Needs of the body are quantitative (physical facilities). Together they create a sync between body and self.
  • Values taught from home frame the self, so we can be in harmony with the self first, then the society.
  • Six values: Human (inculcated from home, basic) · Social (vary with context, depend on others’ judgement) · Spiritual (internalized, not religious, can include nature) · Religious (from home or family, dogmatic) · Ethical (person to person, a catalyst) · Environmental (we get sensitized as we grow older).
  • The self also houses three modes: desire, thought, expectations.
  • Maslow (“Theory of Human Motivation”), bottom to top: Physiological, Safety, Love & Belongingness, Esteem, Aesthetic, Cognitive, Self Actualization, Transcendence.
  • Case study heads: Background, Analysis, Conclusion, Recommendation of solution.
  • Cooley (Looking glass self, “Human Nature & Social Order”): Imagination appearance → Imagination judgement → Self feeling acting upon the judgements.
  • Mead: I (instinctive), Me (conscious, socialized), Self (balances I and Me). Stages: Preparatory (0–2), Play (2–6), Imitation (6 and beyond).

Module 3: Workplace and Ethics

  • Ethics = system of moral principle, professional set up, set by institutions, differs between professions. Ethical values = personal, instilled from families or homes, differ from person to person.
  • Ethics is studied as meta, normative and applied. Ethical values as human, social, spiritual and environmental.
  • Lacuna of ethics: a grave mistake cannot be punished beyond the rule of law or ethics. Ethical values act as a catalyst that speeds up work.
  • Consequentialism (teleological, with eudaimonism): the consequence decides right or wrong. Mill and Bentham. Studied as utilitarianism (greater good for a greater number) and hedonism (pleasure or pain).
  • Deontological ethics (non-consequentialism): Kant. ‘Deon’ = duty. The action matters most. Workplace ethics rests on it and led to canons and codes.
  • Seven canons: Confidentiality (keep strategies sacrosanct) · Diligence (utmost effort) · Integrity (honesty, hard work) · Fairness (salary, promotion) · Legality (punitive action for breaking laws) · Loyalty (to system and clients) · Non-discrimination (gender, nepotism).
  • Code of ethics: compliance-based (laws and policies on hiring, safety, security, dismissal, labour laws) vs value-based (value is the backbone of good will; unfair means hurt the organization).
  • Technology ethics tenets: Accountability, Integrity, Data-privacy, Honesty and fairness.
